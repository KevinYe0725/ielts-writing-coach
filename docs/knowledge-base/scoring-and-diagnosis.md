# Scoring and diagnosis rules

## Score in the official four dimensions

Assess TR, CC, LR, and GRA independently before producing an overall estimate. A strong vocabulary impression cannot compensate mechanically for incomplete task coverage, and a clear position cannot erase frequent grammar errors.

For each dimension return:

- an estimated half-band and honest confidence;
- one or two score-relevant strengths;
- no more than three limiting features;
- exact evidence from the learner's text;
- one observable next action.

## Diagnose before correcting

Use the narrowest defensible category:

- **Hard grammar:** the structure is unacceptable in context, such as `makes children can develop`.
- **Lexical precision:** the selected word does not carry the intended meaning or word class.
- **Collocation/naturalness:** understandable English uses an unconventional combination, such as `slighter pressure`.
- **L1-influenced information structure:** the sentence assembles English words around a Chinese perspective, such as `pressure from the courses`, where English more often attributes workload or pressure to pupils.
- **Logic development:** a claim jumps to a result without a reason, mechanism, example, or qualification.
- **Cohesion:** a relationship or reference cannot be recovered reliably.
- **Optional style:** a valid alternative that improves concision or tone but is not an error.

Do not turn preference into prohibition. `much + comparative` is grammatical in forms such as `much easier` and `much slighter`; the problem in `much slighter pressure` is chiefly collocation and perspective.

## Prioritise for learning value

Rank candidate problems by:

1. likely effect on an IELTS descriptor;
2. recurrence in the current essay or history;
3. transfer value across topics;
4. confidence in the diagnosis;
5. whether one focused paper can create useful output evidence.

Choose one core target. A report may mention other problems, but the paper should not become an unstructured collection of everything that was wrong.

## Confidence discipline

- High: rule-governed, complete context, exact evidence.
- Medium: naturalness, information perspective, or band-boundary judgment with plausible alternatives.
- Low: missing task context, ambiguous intention, uncertain offset, or unstable higher-order judgment.

Low-confidence observations can prompt a check; they cannot alone justify a mastery-state change.
