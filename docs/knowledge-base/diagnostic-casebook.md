# Diagnostic casebook

All cases are original or adapted from the project's own learning examples. They illustrate classification and feedback design; they are not official band exemplars.

## 1. Countability and comparison

**Learner text:** `Children always have a better ability to absorb new knowledges than the elder one.`

**Diagnosis:** `knowledge` is normally uncountable here; `the elder one` does not name a natural comparable group; `always` overgeneralises.

**Useful revision:** `Children generally absorb new knowledge more readily than adults.`

**Transfer check:** compare two groups in a health or technology context without reusing `children/adults`.

## 2. Naturalness, not a fake grammar rule

**Learner text:** `The pressure from the courses in primary school is much slighter.`

**Diagnosis:** `much + comparative` is structurally possible. The real issues are `slight pressure` as an unnatural scale choice, an absent comparison point, and a perspective that makes courses the source rather than pupils the experiencer.

**Useful revisions:** `Primary-school pupils usually face a lighter academic workload.` / `Academic pressure is generally lower in primary school than in secondary school.`

**Transfer check:** express workload from both the institution and learner perspectives.

## 3. Verb-pattern collision

**Learner text:** `which makes children can develop a foreign language mindset`

**Diagnosis:** `make + object` takes a bare infinitive, while `can develop` is a finite modal construction. `foreign language mindset` is also vague and non-idiomatic.

**Useful revision:** `which helps children develop an intuitive feel for the language.`

**Transfer check:** write a result clause with `help/enable/allow` in a different topic.

## 4. Incomplete mechanism

**Learner text:** `Children learn quickly, so early language education is beneficial.`

**Diagnosis:** the conclusion is plausible but the process is missing.

**Useful development:** `Young children are willing to imitate unfamiliar sounds and repeat simple patterns. Regular exposure therefore makes basic structures familiar before formal study becomes demanding.`

**Transfer check:** explain a different education benefit through claim → reason → mechanism → result.

## 5. Connector without relationship

**Learner text:** `On the other hand, children have more free time in primary school.`

**Diagnosis:** `On the other hand` announces contrast, but no prior proposition or explicit contrast is recoverable.

**Useful revision:** either state the contrasted risk first or use a result/addition relationship that matches the paragraph's function.

**Transfer check:** choose and justify the relationship before selecting a linker.

## 6. Optional style versus error

**Learner text:** `Regular exposure therefore helps basic language patterns become familiar before academic work grows more demanding.`

**Diagnosis:** grammatically acceptable and logically clear. `become familiar to children` may make the experiencer more explicit, but this is an optional clarity adjustment, not a hard error.

**Product consequence:** reports must not manufacture corrections merely to appear thorough.
