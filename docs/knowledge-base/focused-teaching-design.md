# Focused teaching design

## Purpose

Focused teaching sits between the detailed correction report and the timed practice paper. Its job is to turn “I can see the correction” into “I know what decision to make next time”.

## Required learning package

For one diagnosed micro-skill, generate a readable Markdown article with 2–6
meaningfully named sections. Select explanation, contrast, worked reasoning,
language tools, or pitfalls according to this learner's difficulty; these are
teaching purposes, not a fixed sequence of cards or a block schema.

State one observable learning goal. Explain when a rule applies, why a plausible
alternative fails, and how to make the decision in a new context. Finish the
article with a brief self-check. Keep 3–4 interactive prompts separate from the
article body, including independent short-text production and unfamiliar-topic
transfer. Reveal references only after the learner attempts the prompt.

The same goal governs the subsequent timed paper, but its questions and answers
must differ from the teaching examples. A call requesting only the article must
not also return the paper or a private planning blueprint.

## Personalisation

- Grammar, spelling, and word form: infer the learning need from the diagnosis,
  then teach through fresh examples. Original-text correction belongs in the
  feedback report, not this tutorial.
- Collocation and naturalness: explain the perspective or word partnership; accept multiple natural rewrites.
- Opinion and argument: teach claim boundaries, reason–mechanism–result chains, qualification, and relevance checks.
- Topic language: teach a small set of high-utility meanings and collocations, not a memorised paragraph.
- Coherence: teach sentence and paragraph function, information order, and reference clarity.

## Quality rules

- The teaching target and paper objective must be the same.
- Examples must be understandable without internal IDs or rubric terminology.
- Do not expose prompts, schema fields, confidence values, model routing, or hidden criteria.
- Do not start the paper timer during teaching.
- Do not reveal any forthcoming paper answer.
- Prefer a decision rule plus varied practice over a long list of “advanced” phrases.
- Tutorial answer feedback must address its stated skill. Do not force a
  causal-chain diagnosis onto an agreement, article, or collocation exercise.
- Distinguish mandatory correction from optional style. Valid alternatives may
  need no change; never fabricate an error to fill an analysis field.
- Authored comparison examples illustrate the rule. Label them as separate
  examples, never as the learner's own answer or the sole acceptable wording.
